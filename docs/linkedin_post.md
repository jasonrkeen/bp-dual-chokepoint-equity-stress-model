# LinkedIn post draft

I recently completed the **BP Dual-Chokepoint Energy Shock Equity Stress
Model**, a companion project to my ExxonMobil compound oil-shock analysis.

The model asks a question that is easy to oversimplify:

> If disruption at the Strait of Hormuz and Bab al-Mandeb raises oil and gas
> prices, does BP necessarily benefit?

Not indefinitely.

I built a four-layer model connecting:

- physical oil and LNG disruption;
- Brent, TTF gas, refining margins, and freight;
- BP's upstream, gas/LNG, refining, working-capital, and net-debt channels; and
- an ensemble equity valuation with 10,000 Monte Carlo simulations per
  scenario.

Using a July 27, 2026 baseline of **$42.86 per BP ADR**, the model produced:

- **$62.50 median** in the Dual-Chokepoint Crisis scenario;
- **$60.40 median** under an Extended Hormuz Constraint; and
- **$28.67 median** at the Demand Destruction Threshold.

The most important output is not the highest valuation. It is the threshold
where higher commodity realizations are overtaken by cargo and volume losses,
freight and security expense, working-capital pressure, recession risk, and
valuation-multiple compression.

The project reinforced an important lesson for me: geopolitical risk does not
move through a company in a straight line. The same disruption can improve
upstream and refining economics while simultaneously weakening demand,
liquidity, and the equity multiple.

The repository includes the model code, transparent assumptions, source
register, scenario tables, sensitivity analysis, threshold map, automated
tests, and an executive PDF.

This is scenario research, not investment advice, and the probabilities are
conditional on each scenario rather than estimates that the events will occur.
